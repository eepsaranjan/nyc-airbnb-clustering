Conclusion

What the segments are good for

K-Means with k=5 was selected as the final model: it delivered the strongest balance of silhouette score and Davies–Bouldin index, scales cleanly to all ~49,000 listings (unlike Agglomerative, which needs sampling), and produces five roughly-sized, easy-to-explain segments rather than DBSCAN's noise-heavy, variable-count output. A held-out 20% split confirmed the same five-cluster structure holds on unseen data.

Practically, the segments separate everyday listings from two more distinct groups worth acting on differently: a small cluster of frequently-booked, high-turnover entire homes that behave like the platform's most reliable inventory, and a cluster of high-price, low-turnover listings run by hosts with dozens of properties each — a likely commercial/professional segment worth different pricing and support treatment than an individual renting a spare room.

Silhouette scores in the 0.24–0.5 range (depending on algorithm and sample) indicate a moderate, genuine cluster structure rather than a clean textbook separation — expected for real-world listing data where segments blend into each other at the edges rather than forming isolated islands.
